# What If Every Home in America Microwaved Popcorn at the Same Second

## Facts (checked)
- 118.65 million of 123.53 million U.S. homes have a microwave oven (EIA RECS 2020, table HC3.7) -> "a hundred and twenty million kitchens".
- A microwave draws roughly 1,000-1,500 W from the wall, like a hair dryer. 119 million x 1.1 kW = ~130 GW.
- All U.S. nuclear reactors together: ~97 GW (94 reactors, EIA). 130 GW > 97 GW.
- The grid cannot store meaningful energy: generation must match demand second by second; U.S. grid frequency is 60 Hz.
- Automatic under-frequency load shedding starts at 59.3-59.5 Hz, i.e. less than one hertz low (NERC PRC-006 and regional standards):
  relays disconnect blocks of customers on purpose to stop a collapse.
- "TV pickup" in Britain: grid engineers follow TV schedules; the record is 2,800 MW on 4 July 1990 when the England v West Germany
  World Cup semi-final penalty shoot-out ended, about 933,000 kettles (Wikipedia "TV pickup", Drax, National Grid).
- Microwave popcorn starts popping after ~45-60 s; frequency relays act within seconds.

## Script (voice-over, English)
If every home in America microwaved popcorn at the same second, the lights would go out before a single kernel popped.

Imagine it's Sunday night. The big game is about to start. And in a hundred and twenty million kitchens, a hundred and twenty million people press start.

One microwave is nothing. About a thousand watts. A hair dryer.

But all of them together? That's more power than every nuclear reactor in America can make. Combined. Asked for in one second.

Wait, doesn't the power company keep some extra?

You see, the grid can't store electricity. Every second, the power plants have to make exactly what you're using. Picture ten people pedaling one giant bicycle up a hill. Now a hundred million friends jump on the back.

The pedals slow down. And the grid has a heartbeat: sixty beats a second. Lose less than one, and the alarms go off.

So giant switches do the only thing they can. They cut off whole cities, on purpose, to save the rest. Including yours.

The crazy part? This is real. In Britain, engineers watch soccer at work. Because the moment a big game ends, almost a million people turn on their tea kettles.

So you'd sit in the dark, holding a warm bag of corn. Zero popped.
