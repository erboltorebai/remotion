# Globzy — "Switzerland: you can't own just ONE guinea pig"

## Facts & sources (to be verified — see fact-check section below)
1. Swiss Animal Welfare Ordinance (Tierschutzverordnung, TSchV, in force 1 Sept 2008): animals of social species must be given appropriate social contact with their own kind. For guinea pigs this means you cannot keep one alone.
2. A guinea-pig rental service exists in Switzerland (near Zurich): when one of a pair dies, owners can rent a companion so the survivor is not alone; when the survivor dies the rented one goes back.

### Claim 1: the law (lines 1, 6, 7). Verdict: TRUE (fair simplification)
- **Art. 13 TSchV (SR 455.1), "Sozialkontakt"**: German: *"Tieren soziallebender Arten sind angemessene Sozialkontakte mit Artgenossen zu ermöglichen."* English (translation): "Animals of social species must be allowed adequate social contact with members of their own species."
- **Guinea-pig rule**: Annex 2 (Anhang 2), Table 1, No. 40 "Meerschweinchen, Cavia porcellus" (enclosure 0.5 m² for 2 animals, +0.2 m² per extra animal), linked to footnotes 39, 41, 45, 47, 54. Footnote 47, as reproduced in the Swiss Animal Protection (STS) guide: *"Die Tiere sind in Gruppen von mindestens 2 Tieren zu halten."* = "The animals must be kept in groups of at least 2." (Exact footnote text not checked on Fedlex.)
- BLV, the federal authority: *"Meerschweinchen dürfen nicht einzeln gehalten werden"* ("Guinea pigs must not be kept alone"), citing Anhang 2, Tab. 1, Ziff. 40.
- Nuance: the law bans **keeping a guinea pig alone**. It does not ban **owning** one. "Illegal to own just one" is the standard press shorthand and fair for a Short. Optional, more precise hook (9 words vs 10): **"In Switzerland, it's illegal to keep a guinea pig alone."**
- Line 6 ("Social pets must have company of their own kind") matches Art. 13. Line 7 ("You need at least two") matches footnote 47 and BLV.

### Claim 2: "So in 2008, the Swiss government stepped in" (line 5). Verdict: TRUE
- The TSchV is dated **23 April 2008**. It was adopted by the Federal Council (Bundesrat), Switzerland's executive government, and came into force on **1 September 2008** together with the revised Animal Welfare Act (TSchG of 16 Dec 2005). "Government" is correct: the Federal Council adopted the ordinance, not parliament. The Parliament-building visual is fine, because the Federal Palace houses both.
- Sources:

### Claim 3: rental service (lines 9–12). Verdict: TRUE (keep the private individual unnamed)
- Widely covered in 2011: a breeder in Hadlikon, near Zurich, rents guinea pigs to owners whose pet has lost its companion. Customers pay roughly CHF 50 for a castrated male or CHF 60 for a female and get half back when the animal is returned, so it is technically a sale with a partial buy-back. When the owner's surviving pig dies, the rental pig goes back to the breeder's herd. That way no owner is left with a new single pig. Swiss consumer magazine Beobachter describes the same "Leihmeerschweinchen" model (give it back after the survivor dies) and names leihmeerschweinchen.ch.
- Caveat: most coverage dates from 2011. Present tense ("can rent") is fine because Beobachter shows the model still exists, but it is not a nationwide official scheme.

### Claim 4: "Guinea pigs are herd animals. Alone, they get lonely, stressed, and sad." (line 4). Verdict: TRUE (colloquial)
- BLV: guinea pigs are social rodents that must not be kept alone; *"Erst in einer Gruppe zeigen sie ihr natürliches Verhalten"* ("Only in a group do they show their natural behaviour"). Peer-reviewed work (Hennessy, Sachser et al.) shows "social buffering": a familiar or preferred companion reduces guinea pigs' cortisol stress response. "Sad" is anthropomorphic but acceptable for this format.
- Sources:
  - https://www.blv.admin.ch/de/nager
  - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4722350 (Hennessy, Kaiser, Tiedtke, Sachser 2015 review)
  - https://corescholar.libraries.wright.edu/psychology/85 (social buffering of cortisol in adult female guinea pigs)
